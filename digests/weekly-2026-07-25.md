### 1. Agent RL Post-Training: On-Policy Distillation, Advantage Shaping, and Trust Regions `[hot,deep,high_value]`

The RL post-training paradigm is converging on patterns that matter: correctness-aware advantage signals (CPO), delta distillation, smooth trust regions (OR Else), and multi-turn prefix replay (ReOPD). These are now named, reproducible recipes that you can apply to improve agent training stability and sample efficiency—particularly for sparse-reward tasks where naive PPO fails.

## Build-of-the-week

**Token-Level Error Attribution for Your Coding Agent**

Extend SWE-Pruner Pro's hidden-state attribution technique to your coding agent: train a lightweight classification head atop your agent's transformer to predict token-level correctness (good/bad/uncertain) on your existing evaluation traces. Use the classifier to rerank best-of-N samples or guide selective re-sampling (TTEL-style). Measure whether token-level credit assignment beats trajectory-level reranking on your task.

- Builds on: **SWE-Pruner Pro (arXiv:2607.18213), TTEL (arXiv:2607.21453), and your existing coding-agent harness (OpenForgeRL, LangChain, or custom). Use your current eval dataset (SWE-Bench, WorkBuddy Bench, or internal) for supervised signal.**
- Prior art checked: SWE-Pruner Pro for hidden-state attribution; TTEL for token-level error localization; CPO (arXiv:2607.14614) for token-level advantage shaping. All three demonstrate token-level credit assignment improves over trajectory-level methods. Also checked: your existing tracker has variance decomposition and dense-reward evals but not token-level attribution for test-time compute.
- Result to publish: Token-level F1 for error detection + best-of-N solve rate improvement (delta vs. trajectory reranking) on your held-out eval set.
- Draft post title: *Token-Level Error Attribution for Coding Agents: Hidden-State Classifiers Beat Trajectory Reranking*
