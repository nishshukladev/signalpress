### 1. Agent Evaluation Infrastructure: Long-Horizon Benchmarks, Metacognition, and Failure-Mode Decomposition `[hot,deep,high_value]`

Six papers plus production case-studies this week converge on a critical gap: existing evals measure binary success on short tasks, missing partial progress, compositional failures, and multi-turn metacognition. Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench, MemOps, DeepStress, AgentCompass, and MedFailBench each decompose agent competence into finer-grained signals (dense rewards, lifecycle operations, robustness under noise, root-cause attribution). This shift from pass/fail to structural diagnosis is what separates weekend experiments from production agent systems—and the tooling is now open-source.

### 2. Security & Trust: Data Exfiltration, Backdoors, and Human-in-the-Loop Fatigue `[hot,deep,high_value]`

Three high-impact disclosures (Cursor 0-day, Grok Build uploading git history + .env secrets, Claude web_fetch DNS exfiltration) plus two research papers on distributed backdoors and undetectable weight poisoning converge into a single message: the trusted context assumption is broken. Coding agents have filesystem access, cloud CLIs demand explicit consent, and multi-agent systems can compose payloads that bypass per-message monitors. Meanwhile, Pydantic's 'the human-in-the-loop is tired' advocates moving to threshold-gated automation with post-hoc audit trails—directly responding to this threat model. Career value: understanding these attack surfaces and designing defensible architectures is now table-stakes for production AI engineering.

### 3. Agent Optimization & Post-Training: On-Policy Distillation, Test-Time Training, and Spectral Rewiring `[hot,deep,high_value]`

Five papers plus two production posts converge on a new post-training paradigm: agents trained with sparse trajectory rewards don't compound improvements (Terminal-Bench 2.0), but on-policy distillation (PUST, Weak-to-Strong, SEED) and test-time training (Self-Guided TTT, RoboTTT) offer concrete mechanisms to close the supervision gap. Spectral Rewiring shows that effective updates concentrate in low-rank spectral subspaces, enabling multi-task merging without catastrophic interference. Career leverage: these are the named practices that differentiate teams shipping reliable agents from those stuck in prompt loops.

## Build-of-the-week

**Dense-Reward Agent Eval: Extend Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench with Your Production Task**

Take one long-running agent task from your backlog (multi-file refactor, research workflow, data pipeline build) and adapt Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench's dense reward-based grading to measure partial progress. Define 3–5 intermediate checkpoints (e.g., 'correct file identified,' 'schema validated,' 'tests pass'), run your agent on 5 variants, and compare partial-success distributions against binary pass/fail. Publish a single-page report with a radar chart showing where your agent drops off.

- Builds on: **Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench (arxiv:2607.08964) + your existing agent harness (e.g., LangChain, CrewAI, or custom) + inspect_ai or braintrust for structured eval logging**
- Prior art checked: Checked Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench paper (arxiv:2607.08964), MemOps (arxiv:2607.12893), AgentCompass (arxiv:2607.13705), and existing tools: inspect_ai, braintrust, langfuse. None offer turn-key dense-reward templates for custom tasks; all require manual checkpoint definition—this build is the minimal viable extension.
- Result to publish: Partial-success rate (%) at each checkpoint vs. final binary success, surfacing where your agent fails in long-horizon execution
- Draft post title: *Where Does My Agent Actually Fail? Dense-Reward Eval on a Production Task*
