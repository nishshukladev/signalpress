## Papers worth a skim

- **[MeanFlowNFT: Bringing Forward-Process RL to Average-Velocity Generators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15273v1)** `[hot,deep,high_value]`
  MeanFlowNFT (arXiv July 16) adapts forward-process RL (DiffusionNFT) to MeanFlow generators, which predict average velocities for few-step sampling. This enables efficient alignment of fast diffusion models with human preferences without reverse-trajectory overhead. Few-step sampling plus alignment is the path to practical, steerable generation; understanding this flow bridges research and production serving.
  Apply ▸ [read] Read sections 1–3 of the MeanFlowNFT paper to understand how forward-process RL applies to velocity-based generators. — via **MeanFlowNFT (arXiv 2607.15273)**
- **[NeuronSoup: Evolving Asynchronous, Shared-Neuron Temporal Graphs without Backpropagation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15217v1)** `[hot,deep,high_value]`
  NeuronSoup (arXiv July 16) replaces synchronous layers with asynchronous, delay-mediated signal propagation through shared neurons, evolved without backpropagation. This architecture explores alternative training paradigms that may scale differently than gradient descent. Non-backprop architectures are emerging as a research direction; early understanding positions you to evaluate alternative training systems.
  Apply ▸ [read] Read the NeuronSoup paper introduction and architecture overview to understand the shared-neuron computation model and evolution-based training. — via **NeuronSoup (arXiv 2607.15217)**
- **[RTS Smoother-Guided Learning of Physics-Based Neural Differential Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15180v1)** `[hot,deep,high_value]`
  RTS Smoother-Guided Learning (arXiv July 16) proposes a hybrid neural-physics ODE framework where known dynamics remain explicit and missing components are learned via neural networks, guided by state estimation. This two-stage method alternates between state inference and parameter learning for partially observable systems. Hybrid physics-ML models are growing in robotics and scientific computing; understanding this pattern prepares you for grounded AI systems.
  Apply ▸ [read] Read sections 1–2 of the RTS Smoother paper to understand the hybrid modeling approach and two-stage training loop. — via **RTS Smoother-Guided Learning (arXiv 2607.15180)**
- **[Partition, Prompt, Aggregate: Statistical Self-Consistency in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15277)** `[hot,deep,high_value]`
  Partition, Prompt, Aggregate (arXiv July 16) tests whether LLM in-context learning satisfies the law of total probability by partitioning prompts and aggregating conditional estimates. The paper introduces 'statistical self-consistency' as an eval framework for probabilistic reasoning. Rigorous eval frameworks for reasoning are scarce; this method provides a principled way to test whether agents perform valid probabilistic inference.
  Apply ▸ [micro-exp] Reproduce the partition-aggregate test on a small dataset using GPT-4 or Claude to validate the self-consistency metric. — via **Partition, Prompt, Aggregate (arXiv 2607.15277)**
